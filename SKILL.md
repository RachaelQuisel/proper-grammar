# Proper Grammar

A comma before "and," "but," "or," "so," or "yet" belongs there only when the words on BOTH sides could stand alone as sentences, each with its own subject. When the second half shares the first half's subject, the sentence has a compound predicate, and the comma is wrong.

Models add this comma constantly. Readers notice it as a reliable sign that an agent wrote the text, so removing it matters even when the rest of the writing is good.

## The test

For each comma directly before a coordinating conjunction, look at what follows the conjunction.

- Does it start with its own subject (a noun or pronoun doing the verb)? It is an independent clause. Keep the comma.
- Does it start with a verb that borrows the earlier subject? It is a compound predicate. Remove the comma.

| Wrong | Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| I read the file, and updated the field. | I read the file and updated the field. | "updated" has no subject; it borrows "I" |
| The automation runs nightly, but skips weekends. | The automation runs nightly but skips weekends. | "skips" borrows "the automation" |
| Open the base, and click Settings. | Open the base and click Settings. | Imperatives share the implied "you" |
| We can rebuild the view, or delete it. | We can rebuild the view or delete it. | "delete" borrows "we can" |
| I read the file, and Mark updated the field. | (no change) | "Mark" is a new subject, so this is a compound sentence |
| It failed twice, so I reran it. | (no change) | "I" is a new subject |

Watch the traps:

- A pronoun that repeats the subject still counts as a subject. "I read the file, and I updated it" is a compound sentence and keeps its comma.
- A phrase tucked in after the conjunction does not supply a subject. "Sharon approved the batch, and after lunch, sent the payouts" still has no second subject, so the comma before "and" is wrong. Move it after "and" so the phrase gets a matching pair: "Sharon approved the batch and, after lunch, sent the payouts." Just deleting it would leave a lone comma after "lunch" that splits the verb from its subject.
- Leave every other comma alone: serial commas in lists, commas after introductory phrases, commas around asides. This skill fixes one error, and pairing the commas around a tucked-in phrase is part of fixing it.
